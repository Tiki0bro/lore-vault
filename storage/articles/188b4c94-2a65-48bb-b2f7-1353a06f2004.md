Known as the "First Enchanter" he is the creator of the first purely enchanted human weapon @[Dawn](item:51e80323-544c-4ef2-8f88-64ca00a86d67). Was able to harnass the power of the sun using a special and lost method that up until now has not been able to be replicated.

Aristol grew up in a small town south of the Capital. He had a high affinty for light magic and was thus sent by his parents out of desperation to study with the Clerics of @[Ethos](person:01ebc462-0f1e-46bb-87b3-f86e08b87be0) where he learned the basic tenants and teachings of the God of Light. He was very bright and a studious scholar. He became quite the adept cleric and understood doctrine and literature quite well. Through his studies of the divine he also became obsessed with magic and trying to understand how magic worked and began dabling in the use of magic, which made the other clerics feel uncomfortable, however he refused to stop even when threatened by some of the senior clerics. Due to not keeping up with the rituals and tenants of his faith he eventually lost his abilities as a cleric and could no longer channel the divine power of @[Ethos](person:01ebc462-0f1e-46bb-87b3-f86e08b87be0), however he continued on his magic research. He became a renowned mage and joined the @Imperium(organization) an organization of wizards devoted to the teaching and studying of magics. Aristol wrote many books about magic and brought with him knowledge of the divine which helped forward the progress of magic. He became obsessed with finding and understanding "Power Words" and how to apply those to objects. He was trying to mimic the gods ways of bestowing "Blessings" upon things, without of course becoming reliant on a "Diety". He made a breakthrough during an experiment and was able to create a @[Sun Stone](item:13820673-ab06-479b-b7f4-be7d5e3b8322) which was an entirely new material he created that was able to harnass the power of the sun. He then incorporated that into his production on what would eventually become the first @Enchanted Weapon(Article). He died shortly after due to a failed experiment which had sent his whole laboratory into a frost. Although his life was cut short, he had begun the Age of progression and many would follow his example and lead humanity forward.

Aristol was both known as a visionary and a heretic and those who branded him a heretic would claim he died as form of judgement from the gods themselves.
